01 / FIELD NOTES

A certificate-authenticated TLS 1.3 exchange

The diagram follows a common ephemeral-DH server-authenticated handshake.

ClientHello offers versions, groups, key shares and signature algorithms. ServerHello selects parameters and contributes a share. HKDF derives handshake keys; the server’s remaining flight is encrypted. CertificateVerify signs a role-specific transcript; Finished authenticates the transcript with a derived key. The client validates trust and service identity before relying on application data. Client certificates, PSK resumption and HelloRetryRequest use additional or different paths.

02 / FIELD NOTES

Different secrets serve different stages and directions

Do not reuse a single raw DH value as every traffic key.

ComponentJobDistinction
(EC)DHE / PQ-T groupEstablish keying materialChosen separately from the cipher suite
CertificateVerifyAuthenticate the transcript under a signing keyNot RSA key transport or bulk encryption
HKDFDerive handshake/application/exporter secretsContext and labels separate stages and roles
AEADProtect records using traffic keys and per-record noncesTLS sequence state and usage bounds still matter

TLS_AES_128_GCM_SHA256 names the record AEAD and key-schedule hash. In TLS 1.3 it does not name the certificate’s signature scheme or the key-agreement group. Forward secrecy is a property of the actual handshake and key lifecycle; PSK-only resumption differs from PSK with ephemeral agreement.

03 / FIELD NOTES

Encryption does not complete endpoint verification

Protocol settings and application semantics affect the result.

  • Validate the chain and intended service name; do not disable certificate verification to fix connectivity.
  • 0-RTT early data can be replayed and lacks the same forward-secrecy properties as later application data. Limit it to explicitly safe operations with replay-aware design.
  • TLS ends at the configured endpoint. A reverse proxy termination is not automatic end-to-end application encryption.
  • Treat session tickets and private keys as managed secrets. Audit negotiated parameters, not only offered configuration.

Read the source: RFC 8446: TLS 1.3 ↗
