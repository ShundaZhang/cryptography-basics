01 / FIELD NOTES

Read history as a change in assumptions

This timeline selects ideas rather than claiming a complete chronology.

CLASSICAL

Substitution, transposition and frequency analysis

Language statistics can survive a transformation. Classical ciphers are useful exercises, not modern confidentiality tools.

19th C.

Kerckhoffs’s design principle

Security should not rely on keeping the algorithm secret. Secret keys and public scrutiny become separate responsibilities.

1910s–40s

One-time pads, machines and information theory

Vernam-style XOR, rotor systems and Shannon’s 1949 secrecy analysis made key reuse, structure and formal secrecy central questions.

1976

Public-key agreement

Diffie and Hellman separated secret establishment from delivery of a pre-shared secret; authentication remained essential.

1977–78

DES and RSA

DES became a public standard in 1977. RSA appeared in 1977 and its journal paper in 1978. Finite key size and scheme encoding became practical concerns.

1980s–90s

Proofs, elliptic curves and deployed protocols

Computational definitions, zero knowledge, ECC and network protocols expanded the goals beyond hiding messages.

2001–18

AES, AEAD, SHA-3 and TLS 1.3

AES was standardized in 2001; SHA-3 in 2015; TLS 1.3 in 2018. Authenticated composition and safer protocol structure increasingly became defaults.

2024–26

PQC standards and hybrid deployment

FIPS 203/204/205 arrived in 2024; RFC 10024 hybrid TLS key agreement in 2026. Migration now includes inventories, formats and interoperability.

02 / FIELD NOTES

The recurring failure patterns

The algorithm can be strong while the surrounding system remains weak.

  • Small key spaces become searchable as computation improves.
  • Repeated pads, IVs or signature scalars reveal structure or secrets.
  • New cryptanalysis changes confidence in assumptions and parameter choices.
  • Protocol oracles, parsing and identity mistakes can bypass mathematically sound primitives.

03 / FIELD NOTES

Compare an original paper with its modern use

Keep historical significance separate from deployment advice.

Read the 1976 Diffie–Hellman paper, then compare its agreement idea with authenticated TLS 1.3. Read the 1978 RSA paper, then study OAEP and PSS in PKCS #1. The gap illustrates why a mathematical operation is not yet a complete secure construction. The Handbook is valuable for mathematics and history, but its older algorithm guidance should not drive current deployment choices.
