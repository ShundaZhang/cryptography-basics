01 / FIELD NOTES

A device-security protocol with explicit roles

This chapter uses published DSP0274 1.4.1; deployed endpoints may support older subsets.

A requester discovers and authenticates a responder, retrieves supported measurements and may establish a secure session. Certificate chains or provisioned raw public keys and PSK modes have different trust setups. SPDM negotiation describes version, algorithms and capabilities; it does not by itself decide whether a device or firmware version is acceptable.

SPDM is not a transport or PCIe traffic cipher

Bindings carry SPDM over a transport such as MCTP or PCIe DOE. Secured-message specifications protect supported session messages; other mechanisms such as PCIe IDE have separate data-path roles.

03 / FIELD NOTES

Authenticate a statement, then appraise its meaning

A valid response from a genuine device can report an unacceptable firmware state.

Check the trust anchor, certificate/raw-key model, expected requester/responder role, negotiated algorithms, response transcript and challenge. Then identify the measurement block type and collection boundary, and compare with approved references. Unknown data, missing mandatory blocks and stale or rollback-prone state need a defined failure policy. Authentication is not authorization to access DMA, keys or resources.

04 / FIELD NOTES

Read libspdm at the message boundary

A reproducible source exercise works without buying a device.

  • Pin a libspdm revision and its supported specification versions. Map requester and responder handlers for one message family.
  • Follow transcript accumulation, signature verification and key derivation. Separate crypto adapters from transport and certificate provisioning.
  • Use project tests or an appropriate emulator to record version/algorithm negotiation, then reject an altered signature or unapproved measurement in your appraisal layer.
  • Document which security decision the reference implementation makes and which decision remains with your integrator policy.
